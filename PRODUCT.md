# Committed

Committed puts a friendly GitHub leaderboard in your Mac’s menu bar, your Windows tray, or an iPhone app.

For contributors, designers, and agents. Last reviewed: 2026-11-10. Positioning and voice guidance live in [Product Marketing Context](.agents/product-marketing.md).

## What it is

Committed is a Mac **menu-bar app**, a Windows **tray app**, and an **iPhone app** for comparing GitHub contributions with friends. Connect GitHub to load the people you follow and members of your GitHub organizations, then hide anyone you don't want on your board.

Open the menu bar (or the app on iPhone) to see where you stand today or over the last 7 or 30 days. Your rank follows the contribution count on your GitHub profile.

## Who it's for

- **Primarily:** developers on Macs, Windows PCs, or iPhones who enjoy friendly competition and want to see how their GitHub contributions compare with friends and people they follow.
- **Also:** teammates who want that same friendly competition with people in their GitHub organizations.

Each user gets a personal board based on their GitHub connections. Friends do not need to install Committed to appear on it; their counts come from their public GitHub contribution graphs.

The leaderboard compares activity counts. It does not assess code quality or work performance.

## Core loop

1. Connect GitHub and let your people load automatically.
2. Open the menu bar to compare contributions for Today, 7 days, or 30 days.
3. Check back as the board refreshes, or turn on notifications to hear when you pass someone or they pass you.

## What it does

Clicking the menu-bar icon opens a popover with your contribution summary and a ranked leaderboard. Each row is a person you've kept visible on your board:

- **Rank + avatar + username** — pulled from real GitHub avatars; your own row is highlighted and marked with a **"you"** badge. Your row never loses your name: when it doesn't fit whole, the "you" badge goes first (the highlight still marks you), then your streak (your summary still shows it), and last your name wraps onto two lines (or, on the small Committed Board widget, shrinks a little). Everyone else's name truncates in the middle. Counts never wrap or truncate: the name gives way first, and only at the largest text sizes does a count shrink a little, still on one line. The iPhone, the widgets, Live rank and Windows follow the same order.
- **Contributions** — the single ranking metric: the GitHub contribution-graph "green squares" count for the selected window. Rows are sorted by contributions, highest first.
- **Your summary** — your avatar, name or handle, contribution count, and streak appear above the list. Your rank also appears in the menu bar when you have visible rivals.
- **Streaks** — the streak mark (a flame with a contribution square at its heart, Committed's own mark, not an emoji) and "12" beside a count is that person's streak: 12 days in a row with at least one contribution. It stays alive through today until today ends, so having no contributions yet this morning doesn't break it. It shows from 2 days, reads "365+" past GitHub's calendar (about a year), and VoiceOver (or a Windows screen reader) reads it as "12-day streak", or "365-plus-day streak" past a year. A count that failed or hasn't loaded shows no streak, never "0". The mark is gray beside the gray number on the Mac, deep lime on the iPhone board, widgets and Live rank (dimmer when their numbers are stale), and lime beside your own streak in the iPhone summary.
- **Today's movement** — small chevrons show rank changes since the first refresh of the day. Hiding or showing people starts a new movement baseline.
- **Trolling** — a day far above someone's usual is marked as trolling: their count turns red and a troll face takes the place of their contribution squares, with "Problem?" and how far over their usual on hover (and for VoiceOver). A day counts as trolling when it's over 100 and more than 5 times their usual big day (the count 9 in 10 of their earlier active days stay under, from the year on their contribution graph). The mark stays on 7 days and 30 days until that day leaves the Window. It doesn't change their count or rank; widgets and Live rank don't show it.
- **Leaderboard Notifications** — opt in through `⋯` to receive a notification when someone passes you or you pass them.

### Time windows

A segmented control at the top switches the window; the contribution count recomputes for it:

- **Today** — the current day.
- **7 days** — the trailing 7 days.
- **30 days** — the trailing 30 days.

Every window is available once the contribution data loads; there is no history-building or backfill period. The app sums the daily counts for the selected range, in the device's time zone.

There is no calendar-month race: no This month Window, monthly Final standings, medals or month-end notification. Committed tried one in development (spec #85) and dropped it before release, because a calendar month and the trailing 30 days answered nearly the same question.

A **week** (Monday to Sunday in your time zone) isn't a Window either. The Board keeps Today, 7 days and 30 days; weeks are used only for the rivalry record, the week records in Your records and last week's final. A finished week is the 7 days Window as it stood at the end of Sunday, so weeks don't bring back the calendar-month race dropped in #85: there is no week-to-date board or week standings.

### Share board

On iPhone and Mac, choose **Share board** from the board header. The image uses the selected time window and the people visible on the board. Choose Board, Your race, Rank, or Top 4 in a 1080 × 1080 Square or a 1080 × 1920 Story. Share the PNG through the system share menu, or use **Save PNG…** on Mac.

### Person view

Tapping someone on the iPhone Board (any row: the list or "Around you"), or clicking a row on the Mac or Windows board, opens their **person view**. On someone else's it leads with their race with you, then their month day by day.

- **Who** — their avatar and username ("you" on your own), with **GitHub profile** opening their profile on GitHub.
- **Your race** (someone else's view only) — **You vs <login> · 30 days**: the Board's 30 days Window, so its totals are the ones the Board ranks. It gives the gap in the Board's words ("5 to pass odile-m", "12 ahead of ezra-q", "Level with ezra-q") and who passed whom: "odile-m passed you on November 8" (plus "the lead changed 3 times" when it did), "You've led for all 30 days", "ezra-q pulled ahead on November 3", "You caught ana on November 9", or "Level for all 30 days". A chart draws both running totals over those 30 days through today, theirs in the contribution green and yours muted, with the latest change of lead marked and each total labeled at its end. Ties count for you, as on the Board, so a level day never counts as being passed. It's a trailing race, not a calendar-month one (see Time windows). It's missing until both your counts have loaded, never drawn as zeros, and VoiceOver reads the card as one line with both totals.
- **Challenge** (iPhone, someone else's view) — a button under **Your race** that opens the share sheet with a 9:16 story image and a message. The image has the board's contribution squares, "A challenge · 30 days", a headline in your voice ("I'm 12 ahead of you, ezra-q.", "You're 12 ahead of me, ezra-q. Not for long." or "Dead level, ezra-q."), a card with both avatars, logins and 30-day totals (the leader's in lime), "Think you can catch me?", and **Committed** with the link "gitcommitted.app/vs/<you>/<friend>". The message matches it: "ezra-q, I'm 12 ahead of you on Committed: <link>", "ezra-q, you're 12 ahead of me on Committed. Not for long: <link>" or "ezra-q, we're dead level on Committed: <link>". The link opens the **Challenge page** for that race. Only the two totals pick the words, as on the page, so equal totals are dead level even though the Board counts a tie for you. It's hidden on the Sample board and until the race has loaded.
- **Weekly record** (iPhone, someone else's view only, under Your race) — your rivalry record against them: wins–losses over the last 12 finished weeks ("7–5"), with your wins in lime. Under it, 12 squares, oldest first ("12 weeks ago" to "Last week"): lime for a week you won, dim for one they won, and an empty outline for a week before either of your calendars starts. The caption gives your longest run of wins among those weeks: "Longest run: 4 weeks in a row", "Longest run: 1 week", or "No wins in these weeks yet". A week runs Monday to Sunday in your time zone and goes to whoever had more contributions in it; ties count for you, as on the Board, even when neither of you contributed. A week counts only once both calendars cover it from its Monday, and this week never counts until it's over. It's shown for anyone, pinned or not, once you both have a finished week, and is missing before then. It's worked out from both calendars each time and never saved, so hiding and showing people doesn't change it. VoiceOver reads it as "You've won 7 of the last 12 weeks against ezra-q" and the longest run.
- **Streak and active days** — one **Streak** tile: their current streak in days ("1 day", "365+ days" past the calendar, read aloud "365-plus days"; "None" once it's broken), captioned with their longest in GitHub's calendar (about the past year) only when it's different ("Best 24 days"), otherwise "Longest in the past year". Beside it, **Active days** this month ("18 of 22": days with a contribution out of the days so far).
- **This month's bars** — one bar per day from the 1st through today, with every day of last month in grey behind them for pace. A bar is selected (today, until you tap or drag to another) and its date and count show above the chart beside last month's same day ("November 8 · 41 contributions", "October 8 · 12"). The legend gives both months' totals.
- **Waiting** — before someone's count has loaded it says "Waiting for <login>'s count" instead of drawing empty bars; if GitHub didn't answer, it says so and that Committed tries again. With a saved calendar and a failed refresh, it shows the saved month under "Refresh failed · showing saved counts".
- **Your records** (your own view on iPhone only, under the Streak and Active days tiles) — your bests over the past year of GitHub's calendar, in a two-column grid captioned "Past year" (one column at the largest text sizes):
  - **Best day** and when ("Today", "Mar 4").
  - **Best week**, Monday to Sunday in your time zone; this week counts so far ("This week", "Nov 2 – 8").
  - **Longest streak** ("Still going", "Ended Oct 18").
  - **Days at #1**: days you had the most contributions on the board as it is now, with ties counted for you ("Most recent: today"). It needs a rival.
  - **Best 30 days**: the best 30 days in a row ("Through today", "Ended Oct 18").
  - **First week at #1**: the first finished week you led the board. Until then it's a dashed "Not yet" tile captioned with where you stand this week ("You're #3 this week").
  - A record set today (this week, for the week records) gets a lime outline and a **New** badge. A record you haven't reached is a dashed "Not yet" tile saying what it needs ("Needs a rival", "Nothing in the past year").
  - Equalling an old best isn't a record, and when several days or weeks tie, the earliest holds it.
  - Days at #1 and First week at #1 count only the people on your board now: hiding or showing someone changes them. VoiceOver reads each tile with its number and when, and says so in their hints.
- **Your own view** adds the private-contributions tip (always shown there, even if you dismissed it on the board) and **What this number can't show**: Contributions count activity, not how good or hard the work was; automated commits and bots count like anyone's; private work counts only when its owner turns on private contributions.
- **VoiceOver** reads the chart's summary ("18 active days this month, most on November 8 with 41"), then each day's bar ("November 8, 41 contributions. October 8: 12"); last month's grey bars aren't read separately.
- **Rival** (iPhone, someone else's view only) — a pin in the bar beside **GitHub**, labelled "Rival", or "Pinned" once on, that pins them to **Your rivals** on the Board or unpins them. Up to three people can be pinned: with three, Rival explains "You can pin up to 3 rivals" and offers no replacement; unpin someone from their view first. VoiceOver reads it as "Rival" or "Rival, pinned". Your own view has none.
- **On iPhone** it's pushed onto the Board, with the back button (or a swipe from the left edge) to return and **Rival** and **GitHub** in the bar. On iPhone Duo open with the panes side by side, it takes the second pane in place of your summary, with **Your standing** to go back and **Rival** and **GitHub** beside it; closing the Duo pushes it again, and opening it again moves it to the pane. It closes if that person leaves the board.
- **Sample board** shows it for everyone, from the made-up calendars.

- **Colors** — their contributions (this month's bars, their race line and the gap) are the brand's green on every platform: lime on dark, deep green on light where lime can't be read. On the Mac, controls (GitHub profile, the "you" badge) keep the system accent like the rest of the Mac app; on iPhone they're lime.

- **On the Mac** clicking a row in the popover opens the person view in its own **Person** window, titled "<login>'s month" or "Your month", like **People…**. There is one: clicking someone else switches it to them, and clicking the person it shows brings it forward. If that person leaves the board, it says to click someone on the board.

- **On Windows** it opens in its own window, with the same content as the Mac's, your own view's tip and **What this number can't show** included.

### Refresh behavior the user notices

- The board **refreshes itself** in the background about every 5 minutes.
- Opening the menu refreshes only if the data is stale (older than ~2 minutes), so re-opening doesn't constantly flash "updating".
- Switching windows shows cached numbers instantly and only fetches when that window's data is stale or missing.
- **Refresh** in the `⋯` menu reloads both the GitHub people list and contribution counts.
- During a refresh, cached values remain visible and **pulse** gently. The first load uses placeholders and a "Fetching the first set of standings" message.
- People-sync status appears on the board and in **People…**. The contribution refresh age appears beside **Refresh** in the `⋯` menu.

### Connecting GitHub and managing people

- **Connect GitHub** runs in a standalone setup window that stays open during the browser hand-off. The app shows a code, you approve it on GitHub, and the token is stored in the Mac's Keychain or Windows Credential Manager. There is no Committed account: connecting authorizes the app's read-only GitHub access on that device, and **Disconnect GitHub** deletes the token.
- After approval, you go directly to the board while your people load. It includes you, accessible members of your GitHub organizations, and people **you** follow. It does not crawl teammates’ following lists.
- Each username appears once on the board, even when you share multiple connections. The people list syncs when you connect or first open the board, then about every 15 minutes during background refreshes. Manual **Refresh** forces a sync.
- **People…** opens a native alphabetical list with search and **All / Following / Organizations** filters. Click the eye to hide someone or the eye-slash to show them again. Hidden people stay in the list so you can restore them. You cannot hide yourself; there is no manual add or remove flow.
- The app loads every page of organizations, members, and follows. Only a complete successful fetch replaces the saved people list. Failed requests preserve that list and show a retry message. On Windows, sources that load successfully can still add people when another source fails; saved people and source memberships remain until a complete sync succeeds. A failed later page contributes no partial list from that source. Organizations that GitHub does not expose to the app cannot appear. An organization that won't list its members for Committed (it restricts third-party app access or enforces SAML single sign-on, so GitHub answers 403 every time) is left out rather than failing the sync, so you and the people you follow still load; the sync status names it and says an organization owner can approve Committed. A 403 that's really a rate limit fails the sync like any other temporary error.
- Visibility choices are saved **locally on each computer or iPhone, per GitHub account**. They survive relaunch, disconnecting and reconnecting, refreshes, and someone leaving and rejoining a source. Hiding someone does not change your GitHub follows or membership. Hidden people are excluded from rankings, contribution refreshes, and crossing notifications.
- When upgrading from the old manual list, the first successful sync saves a local backup (`competitors.manual-backup.v1`). People you still follow or share an organization with retain their cached counts and row identities. People outside those sources leave the active board. Disconnecting clears the active account cache but keeps its visibility preferences.
- Other controls in `⋯`: **About Committed**, **Launch at Login**, **Automatic Updates**, **Check for Updates…**, **Disconnect GitHub**, and **Quit**. **About Committed** explains how contributions are counted.

On iPhone Duo the tabs and toolbar sit in the vertical side bar. Tapping a row opens that person's view (see Person view). Pull to refresh reloads people and contributions. The image shows the Board's current Window and only the people the board shows, with when the numbers are from ("As of Nov 8, 2026 at 1:22 PM") under the widget and "Committed · 27 people" and gitcommitted.app at the foot. **Share** opens the system share sheet with the image alone as a PNG, with no caption.
- **iPhone Duo** — open in landscape, the Board shows two panes divided evenly at the fold: the leaderboard on the leading side and your summary beside it, with the people directly above and below you under it. Wherever there's room for only one (closed, open in portrait, Split View), the leaderboard shows with your summary at the top. Opening and closing keeps the tab and the Window. Centered controls (Connect GitHub, the People filter) move to one side of the fold when it's partially folded like a book.

The section has no footer, so nothing under Streak reminder reads as describing it.
  - **Delivery:** **Right away**, or **Passes now, the rest at 9 PM** (the default). The second holds close calls, records and the streak reminder for the evening summary; passes and results still arrive as they happen. The footer says what each choice does, and names the earlier time when quiet hours start before 9 PM.

  - When iOS Settings has notifications off for Committed, the screen opens with "Notifications are off for Committed" and **Open iOS Settings**.
  - In a build without the relay, on the Sample board, and after **Not Now** to relay notifications, the screen shows only **Passes**, working as before: in the app, while it's open.

### Live rank on iPhone

**Live rank** is a Live Activity that keeps your current rank in the Dynamic Island and on the Lock Screen.

- **Compact Dynamic Island:** the trophy with your rank (for example `#2`) on one side and your Contributions on the other. **Minimal** (when another app's activity shares the island): your rank.
- **Expanded Dynamic Island:** the person ranked directly above you, you, and the person directly below, in the board's own rows (rank, avatar, username, the **you** badge, today's movement chevron, Contributions, and the squares glyph), with the Window ("Today") beside the trophy and, on the right, how long ago the numbers were updated ("13 min, 36 sec"), counting up every second.
- **Lock Screen:** the trophy and the Window with the same counting-up age on the right, then the same three rows.
- Avatars in Live rank are small copies the app saves for everyone on the board each time it refreshes; someone whose avatar hasn't been saved yet shows initials.
- With no visible rivals, Live rank shows your Contributions without a rank rather than `#1 of 1`, and the Lock Screen says "Just you so far", with your streak beside your count (on the Lock Screen) or beside "Just you so far" (in the expanded island). Before your count loads it shows placeholders, never zero, and the Lock Screen says "Waiting for your first count". Twenty minutes after its data, Live rank's colors step down (the lit squares behind a Lock Screen without rows go dark); the compact island, which has no room for the age, shows a clock beside your count.
- The expanded island and the Lock Screen show streaks (the streak mark and "12") on the rows, yours and the people above and below you, as the Board does; the compact and minimal island don't.
- Tapping Live rank opens the Board on Live rank's Window.
- **Settings ▸ Live rank** has an on/off switch, a Window picker (Today, 7 days or 30 days; Today by default, independent of the board's Window), and a status line. When Live Activities are off for Committed in iOS Settings, it says so and links there.
- Live rank starts while the app is open, once you've connected GitHub and your board loads. It updates whenever the app refreshes: in the foreground, on pull-to-refresh, and during background app refresh, which iOS schedules when it chooses. It only updates when what it shows has changed. Hiding or showing someone, or changing Live rank's Window, updates it right away while the app is open. A Window change ends the activity and starts a new one.
- **Disconnect GitHub** and turning Live rank off end it. Only one Live rank shows at a time; extra copies end the next time the app opens.
- **With the Committed relay** (below), Live rank stays on all day with the app closed. The relay recomputes your rank and the streaks Live rank shows every 5 minutes from public contribution graphs, in your time zone's days, and updates Live rank within about 5 minutes of a change. iOS ends a Live Activity after 8 hours, so the relay replaces it with a fresh one at 7.5 hours. If you dismiss Live rank, the relay brings it back at the next 7.5-hour point rather than every 5 minutes. Today resets at your local midnight.
- **The first time Live rank turns on with the relay** (after Connect GitHub, or from the switch), one sheet explains what the relay receives, and that it also sends relay notifications. **Turn On Live Rank** starts it; **Not Now** leaves it off until you turn the switch on, which shows the sheet again. Once accepted, it isn't shown again.
- **Without the relay** (a build with no relay configured), Live rank is on by default and driven by the iPhone alone: iOS ends it after 8 hours, the app can't start one from the background, so it comes back the next time you open Committed, and updates while the app is closed depend on background app refresh.
- **On the Sample board**, Live rank always runs as it does without the relay, whatever the build: it never registers with the Committed relay, the relay sheet never appears, and its settings aren't saved. Leaving the Sample board ends it.

### Widgets

Committed's widgets show Live rank's Window: **Committed** (small, medium and large on the Home Screen; rectangular, inline and circular on the Lock Screen; the small size also shows in CarPlay and StandBy) and **Committed Board** (a small list made for StandBy).

- **Every Home Screen size** is headed by the trophy and the Window ("Today", "7 days", "30 days"), with how long ago the numbers were updated ("13 min, 36 sec") counting up every second on the right. The small sizes put the age on its own line. The medium widget shows the person above you, you and the person below in the board's rows; the large widget shows the top six, with you below a rule when you're further down; the small widget shows your rank and count, with your streak beside the count. Rows on the medium and large widgets show streaks as the Board does; the Committed Board widget's narrow rows don't. The rectangular Lock Screen widget shows "#3 · 12 today" over the age; the inline one shows "#3 · 12 today".
- **Updates:** the app updates the widgets whenever it refreshes. Between those, the widgets refresh themselves every 15 minutes: they re-read everyone's public contribution graph anonymously, as the app does (no token, no Committed relay), re-rank the board the app last saved, carry today's movement on, and work out everyone's streak from the graphs they just read. iOS spends widget updates from a daily budget, so it can stretch 15 minutes to 30 or more; the counting-up age shows exactly how old the numbers are. Someone GitHub doesn't answer for keeps their last count (on Today, until midnight) and their streak until midnight; after that their row shows no streak until GitHub answers. A widget an hour past its data steps its colors down; the age stays grey, since its value already says how old the numbers are.
- **The Sample board's** widgets never fetch: they show what the Sample board saved.
- **Share board** images use the same widgets without the counting-up age; the timestamp under the widget says when the numbers are from.

### The Committed relay

The **Committed relay** is a small push service that keeps Live rank current while the iPhone app is closed, and sends **relay notifications** with Committed closed: a pass notification when someone passes you, or you pass them, close calls, records, a streak reminder, a morning result, and the evening summary. It's the only server Committed uses. It also answers the public rank reads behind **Committed for NZXT Kraken** (below).

- **An iPhone is registered** while Live rank or relay notifications is on, each independently: with only notifications on, the relay never starts, updates or rotates Live rank.
- **What it receives:** your GitHub username, the usernames on your board (visible people, in board order), Live rank's Window and whether Live rank is on, your time zone, whether the build pushes through Apple's sandbox or production service, the Apple push tokens for Live rank on that iPhone, and, with relay notifications on, your notification choices, your pinned rivals, and the Apple token for notifications on that iPhone. Registration uses a random installation ID and secret kept in the iPhone's Keychain. There is no Committed account.
- **Passes** are checked every 5 minutes, after Live rank's updates, with the same rule as the app (`RankChangeDetector`: was at or below you, now strictly above you, or the reverse; holding a tie sends nothing), among the visible people on your board, on the Window you chose (Today by default), and only with your pinned rivals when you chose **Pinned rivals**. During your quiet hours (your local time; they can run past midnight) a pass is still sent, silently and without lighting up the screen (`interruption-level: passive`, no sound). Each sends one notification with the shipped copy ("😤 alice passed you", "They're ahead 7–5. Time to catch up!", or "🎉 You passed alice!", "You're ahead 12–9. Keep climbing!"). Hidden people are never on the board it has, so they're never mentioned. Its first check, and the first after your local midnight, only takes a baseline. The relay keeps a small per-iPhone notification state (the last counts it judged), deleted with the registration. A notification token Apple rejects for good is forgotten until the app sends a new one.
- **Results** (with **Daily and weekly results** on): each morning, yesterday's Today standings, ranked as the Board ranks them among the visible people (a tie counts for you): "You won yesterday" / "41 contributions, 8 ahead of odile-m." ("…, level with odile-m." on a tie), or "You finished #2 yesterday" / "odile-m won with 41. You had 33.". On Mondays, last week's final too, the Monday-to-Sunday standings in your time zone (the same weeks as the rivalry record): "Last week's final: #1" / "186 contributions, 7 ahead of odile-m." (or "Last week's final: #2" / "odile-m won with 190. You had 186."). They go out when your quiet hours end, or at 8 AM with quiet hours off, in your time zone, once each; they wait while your own count can't be fetched. Each is skipped when you have no visible rivals or nobody on the board contributed. Turning results on after that morning's time, or turning notifications back on then, sends nothing until the next morning. The notification state also records the last morning whose results were settled.
- **The streak reminder**, with **Streak reminder** on: "Your 40-day streak" / "Nothing yet today. It keeps going if you contribute by midnight." It's checked in the same 5-minute run and sent from 8 PM in your time zone, or from an hour before quiet hours start when that's earlier (an hour before quiet hours counts only between noon and 8 PM; quiet hours that start in the morning leave it at 8 PM), while your current streak is 2 days or more and today has no contributions. At most one a day, never during quiet hours, and not while your own count is more than 20 minutes old (an old count could wrongly say nothing yet). Tapping it opens your own view. With **Passes now, the rest at 9 PM** it waits for the evening summary instead. The notification state remembers the day it was due.
- **The evening summary**, with Delivery set to **Passes now, the rest at 9 PM** (the default): close calls, records and the streak reminder are held instead of sent, and one "Your day so far" collects them at 9 PM in your time zone, or when quiet hours start if that's earlier (between noon and 9 PM). Passes and results still arrive as they happen. Its body is short lines joined with " · ", for example "#2 today with 41 · New best day: 41 · Close call: 2 to pass odile-m": first where you stand on today's Board, whenever you have a rank, then each record with the day's latest number, each close call with today's gap, and "Your 40-day streak needs one contribution by midnight". Lines are brought up to date when it's sent: a close call you've closed or that's no longer close, a streak reminder you've answered, and a kind you've turned off are left out, and when nothing held is left, nothing is sent. It goes out at most once a day; a close call found after it has gone isn't sent, and a record found then (or during quiet hours) comes in the next evening's, saying "yesterday". Tapping it opens the Board. What was held before switching to **Right away** still comes that evening.
- **What it never receives:** your GitHub token, which is sent only to GitHub. The relay reads the same public contribution pages as the apps, anonymously.
- **The Kraken page's reads** send a username, its rivals, the Window and a time zone with each request. The relay stores nothing from them beyond the shared per-username contribution cache.
- **The challenge page's reads** (`GET /v1/race`, below) send the two usernames in the link. Like the Kraken page's, they're public, unauthenticated, and store nothing beyond that cache.
- **Close calls** (when you turn on **Close calls**) are checked at the same time, on the same Window: you're 1 to 3 contributions behind someone, or up to 5% of their count when that's more (5 behind someone at 100). They're about your pinned rivals, each one you're that close behind, or the person directly above you on the board when nobody is pinned. At most one per person per day (your local day), never during quiet hours: still close when quiet hours end, it comes then. The copy is "Close call: 2 to pass odile-m" / "You're at 31, they're at 33 · Today" (the Window as the Board names it). Someone who passed you in the same check gets no close call that day: the pass already said how far ahead they are. Tapping it opens that person's view. With **Right away** they're sent then; with **Passes now, the rest at 9 PM** they wait for the evening summary. The relay remembers who got one today in the same notification state.
- **Records** (with **Records** on) are checked in the same run, from your own calendar in your time zone, with the same rules as Your records and the record card: a best day, best week (Monday to Sunday) or longest streak is announced once, the first time it's broken in its day or week, and only when the old best is worth beating (a best day over at least 5, a streak over at least 7 days; a best week needs an old best). Equalling an old best isn't a record. A record due during quiet hours waits until they end and then goes out with the day's latest number, saying "yesterday" (or "last week") when its day (or week) is over. One still waiting a day later (say, relay notifications were off overnight) is dropped rather than sent late; a best week can still go out later in its own week. The relay remembers which records it has sent, and any waiting ones, in the same notification state.
- **When the iPhone updates it:** when you hide or show someone, pin or unpin a rival, change a notification choice, change Live rank's Window, when a people sync changes who's on your board, when your time zone changes, and when Apple issues a new push token.
- **Turning off Live rank and every notification switch, or Disconnect GitHub,** deletes the iPhone's registration, with everything the relay holds for it, and ends Live rank. While Live rank or any notification switch is on, the registration stays and the rest keep going.

### Committed for NZXT Kraken

**gitcommitted.app/kraken** puts your rank on an NZXT Kraken cooler's LCD through NZXT CAM's Web Integration. It's a web page, not part of the apps, and needs no Committed app or GitHub connection.

- **Setup:** in CAM, set the Kraken's mode to Web Integration and enter `https://gitcommitted.app/kraken`. CAM shows the page's setup view: enter a GitHub username, load the people that account follows (from GitHub's public API, anonymously), uncheck anyone to leave off, add anyone else by username, and pick **Today / 7 days / 30 days**. Up to 99 rivals. Settings save as you go in the browser's local storage, which CAM shares with the LCD view, so the LCD follows along without a reload. A link with the board in its query (`?login=&competitors=&window=`) works too, for setup done in another browser.
- **The LCD** (CAM adds `?kraken=1`): the trophy and Window, your rank large in lime, "of N", your Contributions, the gap to pass the person ahead ("3 to pass alice", or "You're in first"), and how old the numbers are, over a field of contribution squares. It fits the 640 and 320 round screens and the 240 square one, from the size CAM reports. Empty states match Live rank ("Just you so far", "Waiting for your first count"). Twenty minutes past its data, the colors step down.
- **Counts** come from the relay's `GET /v1/board`, polled every minute: the same public contribution graphs and ranking as the apps, through the relay's shared 5-minute cache. There's no today's movement. Its board is people you follow plus anyone added by hand; organization members and the apps' hide choices don't carry over.

## How it works

The single metric comes from one GitHub source, computed for the selected window:

- **Contributions** — the app reads the public `github.com/users/<name>/contributions` page anonymously, even when GitHub is connected. It sums the daily counts for the selected window locally. Private activity contributes to this public count only when the person has enabled GitHub’s private-contribution visibility setting.

The app saves the people list and loaded counts locally, so relaunches can show saved numbers while new data loads. Rows distinguish loading, cached data, and failed requests. A failed refresh keeps the person's last loaded count, however many refreshes in a row fail: GitHub can fail a very busy account's contributions page for hours. A person with no contribution data shows a placeholder rather than a zero count.

The popover uses native SwiftUI material for a translucent background. It uses an opaque background when Reduce Transparency or increased contrast is enabled.

- **The page:** "You've been challenged", then the headline ("addisonk is 12 ahead of you.", "You're 5 ahead of addisonk." or "You and addisonk are level."), "Over the last 30 days, from both your public GitHub contribution graphs. Updated 3 minutes ago.", and a race card: both 30-day totals ("You" in lime), both running totals over the 30 days with the latest change of lead marked, and who passed whom from the friend's side ("They passed you on November 9.", plus "The lead changed 3 times." when it did; "You pulled ahead…", "They caught you…", "They've led for all 30 days" or "Level for all 30 days", as in the person view's **Your race**). Under it: **Get Committed for iPhone** (the App Store), **Mac** and **Windows** (the site's download links, with Windows "coming soon" until its release), and "You're on addisonk's board. - **Link preview:** the title is "addisonk challenged you on Committed", and the image shows "30 DAYS · 412 – 400" and the challenger's words to the friend ("I'm 12 ahead of you, ezra-q.", "You're 5 ahead of me, ezra-q. Not for long." or "Dead level, ezra-q.") over a field of contribution squares.

- It's `noindex`, and it stays out of the sitemap.

## What it doesn't do today

- **No shared/team board or settings sync.** Each person has their own leaderboard. Connecting the same GitHub account on another Mac, PC, or iPhone derives the same accessible people from GitHub, but visibility preferences remain local. The only server is the **Committed relay**, which holds just what Live rank and relay notifications need on one iPhone (public usernames, Window, time zone, notification choices, pinned rivals, push tokens); it doesn't sync boards or settings.
- **Public contribution graph only.** Private activity is included only if the person enables **Contribution settings → Private contributions** on their GitHub profile. A dismissible board tip explains this; the guidance remains available in **About Committed**. Connecting requests `read:org read:user` to load profile and organization membership information. It does not request `repo` access or read repository contents.
- **No web app.** The leaderboard lives in the Mac menu bar, the Windows notification area, and the iPhone app. The web pages that show counts (the Kraken page and the challenge page) read public graphs through the relay; neither signs in or shows your board. On desktop, standalone windows cover connecting GitHub, People, and the contributions explanation; on iPhone they are tabs and screens in the app.
